I participated in HackHazards’26 with my programming language, **San**, where I was placed in the 51–100 range, and while the exact rank was not disclosed, I was still satisfied with the fact that I defeated 31,900+ developers on my first ever hackathon in my life.

## What I Built  
San is a programming language I started working on about a month before the event. The version I submitted was a fairly complete branch, but it was still behind the current GitHub version of the project. I chose that branch intentionally, since it was more stable for submission, and partly because I had less time back then, and hence could not implement the amazing features I have right now.

## Why I Joined
I joined not because I wanted to win, but because I wanted to see if I stand a chance against real developers, and handle the pressure and competition. Another aspect was the fact that the top prize pool (Ranks 1-5) was enough to get me a laptop, which meant I no longer had to code on android.

## What Went Well  
The biggest win was simply finishing and submitting a working project. I also gained a better understanding of how to balance features, stability, and presentation. Most importantly, I learned that building something original is a very different experience from just studying existing systems. Additionally, I also learnt that presentation is a very important aspect of a hackathon and a project.

## What I Could Improve  
I made a few mistakes that likely kept me out of the Top 10 rank. I could have managed my branch better, which meant giving more time to it and making things better, polishing my presentation better. One big mistake I made avoiding the use of AI implementation and other useless features, since all hackathon projects feature an AI whether it is required or not. I would never shove AI into something that isn't needed but it cost me a few points. Nonetheless, I'd make this mistake again for sure.

## Lessons Learned  
This was my first time, so I expected some rough edges. Still, I had a lot to learn:
- Presentation matters more than I imagined 
- Business & Economical projects are favoured in specific hackathons
- Nobody cares if I made it on android

## Conclusion 
HackHazards’26 was a great learning experience. I did not get the exact rank I wanted, but I built something real, learned from my mistakes, and came away with a stronger project and better direction.

**The 1st rank is mine next time!**
